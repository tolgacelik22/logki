WARN and ERROR have lost their meaning. When every retry, every timeout, every edge case gets logged at WARN, you've trained your team to ignore warnings. The log level becomes decorative. The alert becomes noise.

Domain state is not an operational issue. A payment declined, a user not found, a rate limit hit — these are expected behaviors. They belong in metrics, not in your on-call engineer's 3am pager. When you conflate business logic with infrastructure health, you create false incidents.

False incidents are worse than missed ones. A missed incident teaches you something. A false incident teaches your team to stop caring. After the fifteenth wake-up for a non-issue, nobody trusts the system anymore. Alert fatigue is not a people problem. It's a log design problem.

nois does not page you. It analyzes your log output for semantic consistency, identifies patterns that will become noise, and surfaces the structural issues in your logging before they erode trust. It's a quality tool, not an alerting system.

What nois detects

Semantic log level mismatches

An HTTP 404 logged at ERROR. A successful retry logged at WARN. nois identifies when log levels don't match operational reality — the subtle drift that makes your alerts meaningless over time.

Domain codes logged as warnings

Business logic outcomes — insufficient funds, invalid input, rate limits — should be metrics, not log warnings. nois flags domain-specific error codes that are polluting your operational signal.

Noise hiding real incidents

When 90% of your WARN logs are ignorable, you'll miss the 10% that matter. nois scores log streams for noise density and identifies the patterns burying your actual incidents.

## Finding #1 — HIGH PRIORITY

**payment-service/checkout.go:234**

Classification: DOMAIN_CODE_AS_WARN
Frequency: 4,892 occurrences / 24h

```
WARN: payment declined for user_id=xxx, reason=INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS
```

Analysis: This is expected business logic, not an operational issue.
INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS is a valid domain outcome, not a system warning.
Logging this at WARN creates noise and trains on-call to ignore warnings.

Suggested fix:
- Log at INFO or DEBUG level
- Emit metric: `payment_declined_total{reason="insufficient_funds"}`
- Remove from alerting thresholds

---

## Finding #2 — MEDIUM PRIORITY

**api-gateway/retry.go:89**

Classification: SEMANTIC_MISMATCH
Frequency: 2,341 occurrences / 24h

```
WARN: upstream request failed, retrying (attempt 2/3)
```

Analysis: This is a successful retry path — the system is working as designed.
Logging intermediate retry attempts at WARN conflates transient behavior
with actual failures. Only the final failure (if any) warrants WARN.

Suggested fix:
- Log retry attempts at DEBUG
- Log final success at INFO
- Log final failure at WARN with full context

How access tokens work

After email verification, you receive an access token. This token is a short string that represents your credit balance. Store it locally (e.g., in ~/.nois/token) and pass it to the CLI when running analysis.

Each run decreases your credit count by one. When you purchase more credits, they are added to the same token — no new token required.

Tokens are NOT

  • Kubernetes credentials
  • Service account tokens
  • Cluster access grants

Tokens ARE

  • Safe to store locally
  • Rotatable if compromised
  • Reusable across purchases

What nois never sees

nois NEVER receives

  • Raw log contents
  • Pod names or labels
  • Service or deployment names
  • Namespace names
  • Cluster endpoints
  • Secrets or credentials

nois MAY record

  • Token ID (anonymous)
  • Run count per token
  • Purchase history
  • Email address

All log analysis happens on your machine. The CLI connects to your cluster directly — we never see your infrastructure.
